# Introduction 
Air quality refers to the condition or cleanliness of the air that we breathe. It is determined by the presence and concentration of various pollutants in the atmosphere, such as particulate matter (PM2.5 and PM10), nitrogen dioxide (NO₂), sulphur dioxide (SO₂), carbon monoxide (CO), and ozone (O₃). These pollutants come from both natural sources (like dust storms and wildfires) and human activities (such as vehicle emissions, industrial processes, and burning of fossil fuels).

Good air quality is essential for maintaining the health of humans, animals, and plants. Poor air quality, on the other hand, can lead to serious health problems like asthma, respiratory infections, heart disease, and even premature death. It also affects visibility, damages ecosystems, and contributes to global climate change.

Monitoring and improving air quality is therefore a major environmental priority. Governments and organizations worldwide measure air pollution levels using indices such as the Air Quality Index (AQI), which helps people understand whether the air is safe to breathe or poses health risks.
